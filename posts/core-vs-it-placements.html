Core Engineering vs. IT Placements: A Decision Framework

Every placement season, the same question comes up in the last bench: "Core hai ya IT?" Most answers you'll hear are about salary. That's the wrong first question.

Here's what I actually tell students who ask me this, one on one, after the placement talks are over and the free pizza is gone.

1. Start with what the job asks you to do daily, not what it pays

A core role in manufacturing, power, or design will have you on the shop floor, in front of equipment, solving physical problems. An IT service role will have you at a desk, writing and debugging code, often for a client you'll never meet. Both can pay well. The real fork is: do you want to work with machines and processes, or with software and systems? If you genuinely don't know, that's fine — but decide based on this question first, not the offer letter.

2. Check what each path does to your next five years, not just your first CTC

Core roles in India often start lower than IT service offers, but the growth curve and skill depth can differ a lot by company and sector. IT gives you an easier lateral move into product companies, data roles, or an MBA later. Core gives you deep domain expertise that's harder to build later if you switch away from it early. Ask seniors who took each path where they are three years later — not just what they earned in year one.

3. Be honest about how you performed in the subjects that matter

If your core subjects (thermodynamics, machine design, control systems, whatever your branch's spine is) were where you scored and stayed interested, a core role will use that. If you spent your electives quietly teaching yourself Python and DSA instead, that's a signal too. Don't romanticise core "because it's your branch" if your actual effort went into software all four years.

A short exercise

Before placement season, write down, honestly:

  • The two subjects you enjoyed most, and why
  • Whether you'd rather debug code or debug a machine
  • Where you want to be in five years — a specialist in your branch, or in tech broadly

Match that against the offers you get, not the average package your college WhatsApp group is discussing. The right placement is the one whose daily work you can do well for the next several years, not just the one with the biggest number on the offer letter.
